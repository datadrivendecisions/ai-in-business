The documents that specify your platform

One document a week, one to three pages, posted in your team's Teams channel. Questions come back. A mark never does.

Your handbook is judged on the research. Your platform is judged on whether it works, and the evidence for that is six short documents, one a week. Together they say what you are building, how it is built, and how you know it works.

This page lists the six in order, says what happens to a document after you post it, and says what stays with you. The deadline for each one is on its week page.

The criteria come first. Each document is read against a list published on this site before the deadline. The PRD's list is up. The others arrive with their week page, about a week ahead, like everything else here.

The six, in order

Each document is set in its week's session and is due before the next one. The order is fixed, because each one needs the one before it. A blueprint implements a PRD; a build plan follows a blueprint. What each document must contain is set on its week page, and the bar it must clear is one sentence, given below.

Weeks 3 to 6 are the plan, and the plan is revisited weekly. The order will not change; the detail arrives a week ahead.

What happens after you post it

The same six steps every week. Nothing in them is a mark, and nothing in them can stop you.

  1. Read the criteria first. They are on this site before the deadline, and they are the same list the tutor is given. Hold your draft against them yourself before anyone else does.

  2. Post the document in your team's Teams channel by the deadline on the week page. Post the file itself: PDF, Word or Markdown. A link to a single page on GitHub also works. A link to a whole repository, or to a Google Doc, cannot be read and counts as not handed in. One document per team. If you post a second version in the same week, the latest one is read.

  3. A lecturer hands it to the AI tutor. The tutor reads it against the published criteria. From week 2 it also reads it beside every document your team has posted before, and asks whether they still describe one platform. A blueprint that quietly drops something the PRD promised is exactly the kind of thing it finds. It reads your team's documents and nobody else's.

  4. Three to five questions come back in the same channel, sent by the lecturer. Every message opens the same way: these are questions, not feedback; nobody is grading this message; there is no answer key behind it. If the document missed something the brief plainly asked for, one note says so before the questions, and it is marked as feedback.

  5. Take the questions into the next document. No reply is expected. The tutor remembers what it asked. A question you answered is not asked again. A question you left open comes back, and one you dodged comes back harder. Rejecting a question with a reason closes it as surely as answering it.

  6. Write it into your portfolio the same week. The questions and what you changed answer the third of the four standard questions; the document itself answers the fourth. The platform and the documents that specify it are part of what your team hands in at the end.

What stays with you

The tutor reads what you post

Nothing else. It does not look into your repository, your machines or your channel. Everything it knows about your platform is in the documents you chose to hand in.

No score reaches you

The teaching team reads the same document against the same criteria, to prepare the session. That reading is not a mark, and nothing of it reaches you as a number. What you get is the questions.

Team numbers, never names

Your document is filed under your team's number. No student's name goes to the tutor. No student document ever enters the public repository or this site.

Interview material stays out

Recordings, transcripts, and the names of people at your partner firm never go in these documents, and never go through the tutor. They stay inside HAN's own Teams, where your team keeps them.

They change, and they are meant to

Each document is one to three pages, and it lives. The PRD you wrote in week 1 will be wrong by week 3, because you will know more. When the blueprint shows the PRD promised something the platform cannot deliver, change the PRD and say why. Give the new version a number and a date, so that anyone can see which one they are reading.

From week 3 your team keeps a decision log for exactly this: one entry per choice, saying what failed and what you changed because of it. It travels with the first evaluation in week 5, and it is what the final interview walks you through.

Short beats long. A one-page document that clears its bar beats three pages that do not. And an open question you name, with an owner and a route to an answer, is worth more than a filled section that asserts.

Where this comes from

The sequence of six, and the rule that criteria are published before they are used, come from the course's own design records. Those are in the repository. The Socratic tutor is the same one that questions your published page each week; how a week runs has both gates in full.

None of this is the criterion set your AEL mark is given against. That is separate, and it is published before it judges anything.
