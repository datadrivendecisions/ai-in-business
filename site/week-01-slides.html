3 / 13

How this module runs

  • One pipeline, two modules. AEL builds the tool. AIBS decides what its output must achieve, and judges it.
  • Teams of four, both modules. Every team works together on the AEL and AIBS assignments; each student also holds one AEL build role.
  • The goal: a Handbook about the state of AI, aimed at SMEs — what a regional owner needs to know and decide.
  • The rhythm: one new published page per team, per week.
  • The scope: 6 weeks of classes, then one combined assessment for both modules.

Your experience with AI

This is also our diagnostic — it shapes what we do in the coming weeks.

  • Which AI tools have you actually used, and for what?
  • Where has it clearly helped you? Where has it failed or misled you?
  • Confidence check: using AI · building with AI · judging AI output
  • How will AI change the job market you're about to enter?
  • What skills will you need — which do you have, which are gaps?
  • What do you want to be able to do by the end of this minor?
Discussion 2
7 / 13

The Draghi report

  • What it is. Mario Draghi's report for the European Commission (September 2024) on why the EU is falling behind the US and China.
  • Core diagnosis. The gap is largely a technology gap — and Europe is slow to diffuse digital tech into existing businesses, especially SMEs.
  • On AI. Europe won't win only by building frontier models; it has to get AI adopted across its industrial base.
  • SME barriers. Fragmented single market, regulatory complexity, limited capital access, skills shortages, limited scale.
  • Why it's our starting point. The Handbook is a small, concrete contribution to exactly that diffusion problem.
Discussion 2
8 / 13

Guided discussion

  • Do you recognise the “Europe is behind” framing? Who makes the AI tools you named earlier?
  • Draghi treats the productivity gap as a tech gap. What would closing it actually take for one local SME?
  • If you were advising a regional SME tomorrow, what's the first thing they'd need to know about AI?

Assignments
10 / 13

AEL — Product Requirements Document

  • Problem and users. What the platform is for, and who uses it — your research team, and, downstream, the SME owner.
  • What it must let a user do. The path from raw source material to a published handbook page.
  • Output qualities. What a good handbook page produced by the platform looks like.
  • Known constraints. You direct an agentic CLI rather than hand-code; reuse existing infrastructure; a non-coder must be able to run it; every step needs a manual fallback.
  • Out of scope, for now. What you are deliberately not building yet.
  • Open questions and assumptions. What you're still unsure about.
Assignments
11 / 13

AIBS — Research Proposal

  • Target audience. Which SMEs is this for? Be specific — sector, size, region. A named partner company is ideal.
  • What they need. What these SMEs need to know or decide about AI, and why — use the Draghi report and your own reasoning.
  • Research question. One main question + at least 4 sub-questions — at least 2 on external analysis, at least 2 on internal analysis.
  • Quality criteria for the handbook. What must it do to be genuinely useful to this audience?
  • Methodology. Desk research + field research — one SME visit, weeks 2–4. Which questions does each method serve?
Assignments
12 / 13

1–3 pages each. This is a starting point.

  • PRD, the bar: a reader can tell what you're building, for whom, and how you'll know it works.
  • Research proposal, the bar: a reader can tell who the handbook serves, what it must answer, and how you'll judge it.

Push for a concrete target SME — sector, size, region — as early as possible.

You'll revise both as you learn more. Nothing here is final.
