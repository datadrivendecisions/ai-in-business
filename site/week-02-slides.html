Your PRDs — nothing in them could be proven wrong

Eight drafts, twenty criteria. One missing thread, in three places.

  • Before — a pain point with evidence. “Researchers have to manually search for information, evaluate sources, translate Dutch material … This takes a lot of time.”
  • Claim — a hypothesis you could be wrong about. “The overall goal is to make the research process faster, more consistent and accessible to every team member.”
  • After — how you will know it works. “The platform is successful when the research team can consistently produce one reliable and reviewed handbook page per week.”

A blueprint says what the parts are, not how to code them

  • Implements your PRD. One to three pages.
  • Done when a reader can tell: what each part does, what passes between them, where the line is
  • Boxes and words. The developer holds the pen; the whole team reads it.

Assignment 1
11 / 15

Report writing with AI

  • Three prompts, separately. Ask AI to argue the US dominates AI. Then, in a new conversation, that China dominates. Then Europe.
  • Don't cross-reference. Each article should be written as if it's the only one AI is producing.
  • Read them back to back. What do you notice?
Takeaway
12 / 15

AI gives you what you ask for

  • Not what's true. Ask for the US case and you get a convincing US case — same for China, same for Europe.
  • Source diversity is the fix. A cleverer prompt will not tell you who leads.
  • This matters twice. For your business judgement (informed, unbiased) and for you personally (know your own starting position).
Assignment 2
13 / 15

Search strategies and quality criteria

  • The question. Good-quality reports on the geopolitics of the AI race — US, China, Europe.
  • Your criteria, explicit. What makes a source “good quality” here? Write it down.
  • Your strategy, explicit. How did you search, and why?
  • Then, AI — carefully. A first analysis of your sources with AI, with your strategy made explicit too.
Way of working
14 / 15

Peer review and the Socratic agent

  • Partner teams. Every team pairs up with another team to review each other's work.
  • A Socratic agent. Every team's work also gets questioned by an AI tutor — informational, not scored.
