Instructor notes

Session of Monday 12 October, the last of six. Interviews with the firms are still running for some teams, so avoid calling anything "the interview" without saying which one. The next stop is the final assessment: the hand-in on Sunday 25 October and the final CBI on Monday 26 October.

Four parts. Start warm, with what the firms said. Then the AEL lecturer. Then the practical part: what goes where and when. Close with the discussion, which is the fun part and the first rehearsal for guiding question 3.

Invite the teams that have held their interview with the firm to share. Two minutes each, three prompts on the card. Teams without an interview yet: listen for what they can still ask, or use for their plan B. Keep it positive; where fieldwork is still behind, the point is what each team can still do in two weeks.

Two consequences of the interview. The results go into the handbook pages, which teams may keep improving until the hand-in on 25 October. The transcript goes into each student's portfolio. Consent and the rules on interview material still apply: the transcript goes to HandIn, not into the public repo and not into the LLM wiki.

AEL slot: the evaluation of the LLM wiki. The AEL lecturer fills this card in, or uses it to take the floor.

The timeline. Three dates, nothing else. The hand-in deadline is Sunday 25 October at 23:59.

Two uploads on HandIn, by every student. HandIn still carries the old module names: the video goes under Data Mining, the portfolio under Business Intelligence. The video is one per team, and each member uploads the same file or a link to it. Full details: the final interview page, section "Before the interview".

The portfolio: the team's four handbook pages, the interview transcript, and a link to the team's GitHub repository. The video does not go in the portfolio again, but the repo should link to it.

Takeaway. Every student is questioned individually. If the portfolio says you did something, expect to be asked how.

On 26 October: 20 minutes per team, both lecturers, in English. About five minutes of pitch, the rest is questions to each student in turn. Slots are on the final interview page; no sign-up; be there five minutes early.

The four guiding questions, one line each. Prepare all four. The full wording is on the final interview page.

Two grades, one per module. Each is 70 % final CBI and 30 % portfolio. The mini-CBI in week 4 was diagnostic and did not count.

Takeaway. No late hand-in is possible: the lecturers start watching the videos on Monday morning at nine.

Recap of last week's in-class assignment: front-runner AI use cases in each team's sector, in one of three regions. The postcards from 2035 became homework: each team posted its postcard in its Teams channel or GitHub repo before this session.

Each team pitches its postcard and its strongest case in about two minutes, using the five questions from the assignment. Group the pitches by region so the differences show. This is the starting point of the discussion.

From the cases to the choices: how do the choices we make now shape the economy, and the AI-powered jobs in it? Suggested format: agree on one side of the room, disagree on the other; one student per side gives a reason from their case; anyone may switch sides after hearing the other.

Ten statements follow (s17–s26). Pick the ones that fit the room; there is no need to do all ten.

Statement: AI will make small firms stronger against big companies.

Statement: Europe should accept slower AI adoption in exchange for stronger rules.

Statement: by 2035, most SMEs will employ fewer people because of AI.

Statement: whoever owns the AI models will own the profits of the economy.

Statement: depending on American or Chinese AI is fine for a Dutch SME.

Statement: time saved by AI should become a shorter working week.

Statement: AI hits entry-level jobs first, so your first job gets harder.

Statement: governments, not markets, should decide where AI may replace workers.

Statement: an SME that does not use AI by 2030 will not survive.

Statement: AI makes work more human, with machines taking the routine and people the rest.

The rulebook. Rules 1 to 3 from weeks 1 to 3; rule 4 from week 4, "Technology is not the barrier"; rule 5 from week 5, know your known and unknown knowns and unknowns. Rule 6 is added together in this session.

Close. See you on 26 October.
