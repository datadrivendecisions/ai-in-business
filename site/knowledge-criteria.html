What a good knowledge architecture looks like

A reader can tell where your sources and drafts live, how the platform finds them again, and why you chose that way.

That sentence is the bar the week 3 assignment sets. This page unpacks it into the twenty things a reader looks for. Hold your own draft against them before anyone else does.

The criteria come from three places. The first is the five things the assignment asks for. The second is your blueprint, which this document extends rather than replaces. The third is how practitioners judge a store of documents a machine has to search. Each criterion says where it comes from.

Posted already? Nothing is lost. If you post a second version before the deadline, the latest one is read. The feedback on your knowledge architecture uses these criteria and nothing else. Your marks are given later, against separate criteria.

Three things that are different here

This is the document that decides what your platform can honestly say. Three things follow from that, and they are not obvious.

A choice, not a tool

Naming a database is not an architecture. The choice is how a question reaches the passage that answers it. Look up the passage when the question is asked, or write the pages in advance: both work, and each costs you something.

A store can be a folder

A named folder of files with a naming rule is a real answer, and for a platform this size often the right one. What is read against you is whether a reader can find one passage in it, not how modern it sounds.

Where a claim came from

Your handbook page has to show its sources. That is only possible if the source travelled with the text from the moment it entered. This document is where that either happens or quietly does not.

The twenty criteria

Group A is what the platform has to know. B is where it lives and how it is found. C is why this way and not another. D is the line from your blueprint, kept. E is the trace back to your earlier documents, and F and G are about the document itself.

A · What the platform has to know

  • A1 — Every kind of material, listed. Sources, your own drafts, the context file from week 2, interview material, anything else the platform reads. One line each, and a reader can tell them apart. Assignment ask 1
  • A2 — Where each kind comes in. For each kind: who or what puts it there, and in what form. A source someone pastes in by hand is a fine answer, stated. Assignment ask 1; blueprint A1
  • A3 — What travels with a source from the start. The fields kept beside each source: where it came from, when it was read, who chose it. Enough to write a reference on your page from the store alone. Week 2: every claim needs a source you would defend; data lineage practice
  • A4 — What the platform does not keep. One sentence on what stays outside the store, so the reader can tell a gap from a decision. Interview recordings are the obvious case. Assignment ask 4; ADR-0013 on field material

B · Where it lives, and how it is found again

  • B1 — The store, named. The place the material sits: a folder, a repository, a database, a spreadsheet. Named well enough that a team member could open it today. Assignment ask 2
  • B2 — How a thing is named and found. The rule that makes one file findable among two hundred: the naming pattern, the folders, the index, or the field that is searched. Rosenfeld & Morville, Information Architecture
  • B3 — The path from a question to the passage. Trace one real question your platform will be asked, from the question to the passage or page that answers it. Every step named, including who or what does the looking. Assignment ask 2
  • B4 — What happens when the material changes. A source is replaced, a draft is revised, a page is republished. Say what is updated and what is left alone, so nobody reads a stale passage without knowing. Records management practice; week 5 evaluation

C · Why this, and not the alternative

  • C1 — One alternative, weighed. The other way you could have done it, and what it would have cost or saved. An alternative set up to lose has not been weighed. Assignment ask 3; architecture decision records
  • C2 — The promise it serves. The choice follows from the job your platform has. Point to the promise in your PRD that this architecture is for. Assignment ask 3
  • C3 — What would make you switch. The thing you would see — a size, a wrong answer, a week lost — that would send you back to the alternative. Assignment ask 3

D · The line, kept

  • D1 — Where the protected material sits. Your blueprint names a line nothing may cross. Say where the material on the protected side is stored, and that it is a different place from the rest. Assignment ask 4; blueprint C1
  • D2 — What stops it being read out. The thing that keeps protected material out of a call to a model service: a person who carries it by hand, a folder nothing reads, a check at the door. A rule nobody applies is not a stop. Assignment ask 4; blueprint C2
  • D3 — Text from the web is material, never an instruction. A fetched page can contain sentences written to steer a model. Say where web text enters your store and what stops it from giving orders. OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications, on prompt injection

E · The trace to your earlier documents

  • E1 — Which parts read and write the store. The parts from your blueprint, named, with what each one reads and what it writes. A store nothing writes to stays empty. Assignment; blueprint A1 and B1
  • E2 — Departures recorded. Where this document changes something the blueprint or the PRD said, it says so, and the earlier document gets a new version with a date and one line of reason. Platform documents: they change, and they are meant to

F · Decisions and open questions

  • F1 — The decision log's first entry. This architecture is entry one: the choice, what it rules out, and what would reopen it. The log then runs to week 5. Assignment; week 3 page; ADR-0010
  • F2 — The tutor's questions, answered. The questions your team was sent about the blueprint, each with a short answer: what you changed, or why you left it. A question you reject with a reason is closed. Assignment ask 5

G · The document as a working instrument

  • G1 — Header and provenance. The platform's name; the team; who held the pen; a version and a date; and which versions of your PRD and blueprint it extends. Platform documents
  • G2 — The three-question test. From the document alone, in a minute each: where does the material live, how is one passage found, and why this way? This is the assignment's own bar and the only item read as a whole. Assignment

What is not read against you

Not read against you: which tool you chose, how modern it is, length within the one to three pages, drawing style, prose quality, and any code. A named folder with a naming rule, traced end to end, beats a vector database nobody can search by hand.

A drawing inside a PDF or Word file may not survive being turned into text. If your drawing matters to a criterion, say in words what it shows as well. Every drawing is also looked at by a lecturer.

Two things are worth doing already, though nobody asks for them this week. They come back in week 5, when you evaluate what the platform produced:

  • A record of each lookup. Which question, which passages came back, which one was used. Without it you cannot tell why a sentence on your page is wrong.
  • A check that a quoted passage is really in the source. A model can produce a quotation that was never written. Matching it back to the stored text is a check a script can do.

Where this comes from
