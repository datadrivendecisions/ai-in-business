Week 4 — show us what you built

Content is easy to produce with AI. We want to see what you did with it: what you tried and tested, what went wrong, and what you changed.

Week 4's session is a mini-CBI: a short version of the criterion-based interview that ends the course. Your team shows the work of the past week and answers our questions about it. The questions are below, so you can prepare.

Week 4's theme on the week map is SME knowledge gaps. What does a regional SME not yet know about AI, and what would it need to know to decide well? It is the theme of the assignments set in this session.

The mini-CBI

Each team has a short interview with the lecturers. It covers three things from the past week: the first version of your desk research tool (your V0 build), your team's new handbook page, and your interview guide.

The length of each slot, and whether the mini-CBI counts towards your mark, are confirmed in the session.

The questions we will ask

  1. Demo. Give a short demo of your desk research tool, and show us the handbook page you wrote for it. What does the tool do, and where does it still fall short?
  2. Why it's agentic. Explain what makes your tool agentic, using the concepts from class: the LLM, its skills, its tools, and the context you give it (PRD, research proposal, blueprint, build plan). Which of these made the biggest difference?
  3. From question to interview. How did you turn your research question and sub-questions into interview questions? Did writing the interview guide make you change your research proposal? What changed, and why?
  4. What you learned by getting it wrong. Tell us about something that didn't work this week: an approach, a prompt, or an output from the AI that you rejected. How did you find out, and what did you do about it?
  5. Field research → tool. How do you expect your interviews to help you improve your desk research tool?

If you read this too late to fit it into your presentation, don't worry. If you've worked seriously on this week's assignments, you'll have plenty to tell us. Honest answers about what didn't work count as much as polished ones about what did.
