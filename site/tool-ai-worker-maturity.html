A measuring device, with its faults marked

Every AI maturity model measures the company.

This one measures a person. It places you on six levels using facts you can check — not statements you agree with — and then names the single thing standing between you and the next one.

It is assembled from three separate findings that had never been put together: a stage ladder whose value sits in the middle, a two-axis structure where the axes compound, and one worker's narrated climb with observable markers along it.

The levels are not about which tools you have. They are about who holds the context, who writes to your systems, and where you keep your judgment.

Instrument status — read before using

This is a defensible composition, not a validated instrument. Its ladder shape is borrowed from an enterprise model built on a 2022 survey. Its two-axis structure is transposed from an organisational construct measured across 3,467 respondents. Its level markers come from a single narrated case — self-reported, unaudited, and selected for being worth broadcasting — plus one population-scale measurement of learning effects.

That is enough to locate yourself and pick a next move. It is not enough to rank people, gate a promotion, or put a number in a performance review. Treat a result as a conversation starter with yourself.

v2 changes, after scoring three real cases. Five defects in v1 were found by running it against people rather than reasoning about it: one gate conflated a practice with an artifact and sat a level too high; one gate asked you to rate a capability instead of report a behaviour, and could collapse the whole ladder on its own; one gate's wording covered scheduled work but not event-triggered work; the two axes were measured with 4 questions against 9; and two gates were near-duplicates. All five are fixed. The full record, including the cases and what changed because of which, is in the calibration report.

Why two axes and not one

You can be fluent and still immature

The strongest argument against a single score comes from a 3,467-respondent study that scored organisations on two learning capabilities at once. Either one alone helped a little. Having both compounded — up to 2.2× on some measures. The majority, 59%, were low on both. The axes below are that finding transposed to a person.

Axis F

Fluency & judgment

Can you get good work out of the model, and — the part that actually matters — can you tell when it hasn't? This is measurable and learnable: after controlling for task type, model choice and complexity, longer-tenured users show systematically higher task-success rates.

The terminal skill on this axis is not directing. It is evaluating — knowing what good looks like precisely enough to check the work.

Axis S

Work-system design

Have you restructured your own work so the AI has context, reach, and somewhere to put its output? This is where the compounding happens, and it is why someone can write excellent prompts all day into a stateless chat window and still capture a fraction of what's available.

Most of the observable markers live on this axis, which is why the assessment asks more questions about it. Facts about your setup are checkable; feelings about your fluency are not.

The assessment

Sixteen gates

Answer from what is true this week, not what you intend to set up. Every question asks about something observable — the surest way to get a useless result is to answer aspirationally. Each level is a gate, not a score: you are at the highest level whose gates you have all passed.

Provenance

Where each piece comes from

Nothing here is invented. Each part of the model is traceable to a page in the wiki, and the full audit trail — including the 65 pages the query considered and rejected, with a reason for each — sits alongside this file.

The ladder shape

MIT CISR's four-stage enterprise model (28 / 34 / 31 / 7%), and its finding that the greatest financial impact comes between stages 2 and 3 — not at the top. sources/2026-04-28-mit-sloan-ai-maturity

The two axes

Ransbotham et al., MIT SMR × BCG, n=3,467: two learning capabilities scored independently, compounding when combined, 59% low on both. sources/2026-05-07-ransbotham-augmented-learners

The level markers

The corpus's only narrated end-to-end worker trajectory: share of work in one harness, who writes the system of record, agent-detected context gaps, friction logging, distribution. sources/2026-08-31-blum-how-i-ai-claude-cowork-pm-system

That fluency is learnable

Anthropic Economic Index 5: high-tenure users show higher task-success rates after controls, and model selection tracks task value. sources/2026-05-07-anthropic-economic-index-5-learning-curves

The L4 boundary

"Delegate tasks, not judgment." Google's Developer Intelligence team on the five patterns of top AI-native engineers, and verification as the new bottleneck. sources/2026-04-21-forsgren-macvean-build-core-skills-thrive-ai-era-developer

The trough

The productivity J-curve at individual scale: return runs negative first, trust falls, you double-check everything. A snapshot cannot tell a climb from a failure. concepts/micro-productivity-trap

What is maturing

Judgment and evaluation, not tool knowledge — evaluation named the terminal skill for people managing fleets of agents. concepts/durable-skills

Why it doesn't sum

A company of Level-4 individuals is not a Level-4 company. Distribution turned out to be a UX problem: three days by terminal failed, fifteen guided minutes worked. concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption

The gap this fills

All four maturity instruments in the wiki measure firms; success rates span 1–13%. The worker-level half of that thread's question is now answered by the wiki synthesis; external validation is still wanted. threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison
