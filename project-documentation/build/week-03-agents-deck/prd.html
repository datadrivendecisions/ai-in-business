PRD: the AI agents segment

By the end of fifteen minutes a student can say what an agent is, what the harness around it does, and on which of three counts an SME can trust one.

Three cards that introduce AI agents in the week 3 session. By week 3 every team has already used an agent: the agentic CLI of the AEL week 1 spike. The segment gives what they did a name and a literature, and links it to the week’s two themes: trust in AIBS and the knowledge-architecture decision in AEL.

1 · Problem and users

The problem. Students have used an agent for two weeks without a shared word for it. The AEL spike put an agentic CLI in their hands; the week 2 debrief named the harness and ReAct. What nobody has done yet is put the pieces side by side: what makes something an agent, what the harness around it is for, and what that means for the SME a team is writing about. Week 3 needs exactly that vocabulary twice. The AIBS theme asks whether an SME can trust AI. The AEL decision asks where the team’s knowledge should live.

Users.

  • The lecturer who presents it, from the presenter deck, with a note under each card.
  • The 32 students in the room, eight teams of four, predominantly business students with no coding background. They listen; they do not read.
  • The same students afterwards, on the card page on a phone, following the links to the wiki and the two visualisations.

2 · What it must let a user do

2.1 Card 1 — name what they have already used

A student can say what an agent is in one sentence. Material, with the wiki page each point rests on:

  • An LLM with tools running in a loop to accomplish a goal — the definition Google and Anthropic developer material share by mid-2026 (concepts/ai-agents). Wooldridge’s older definition leaves the LLM out (sources/2009-01-01-wooldridge-…).
  • The line between a workflow and an agent is who decides the next step: code, or the model (sources/2024-12-19-anthropic-building-effective-agents).
  • The loop they watched in week 1 is reason → act → observe (concepts/react-reasoning-acting).
  • The intern analogy carries through (LRD Appendix A.1; Karpathy’s intern entities on concepts/ai-agents).

2.2 Card 2 — see the harness, and this week’s decision

  • The harness is the software around the model; the model is the CPU and the harness the operating system (concepts/agent-harness). The Harness is the OS visualisation shows it.
  • This week’s AEL decision: where knowledge lives. RAG retrieves, an LLM Wiki compiles, Fat Skills act (syntheses/knowledge-architectures-for-llm-agents). The Four ways to answer one question visualisation shows the same question answered four ways.
  • The three layers still ahead — Contracts, Constraints, Compounding — appear as names only. The LRD makes them debrief reading, read after the failure each one answers.

2.3 Card 3 — trust, on three counts

The week 3 AIBS brief asks one question with three answers: can the SME trust AI with its data, its output, its people?

  • Data. The model is rented and the harness owned (concepts/agent-harness); a small language model is defined by the device it fits on, which makes when does local win answerable (concepts/small-language-models).
  • Output. Users regret an agent that acted beyond what they would have authorised, even when the result was right. The trigger is irreversible and externally visible actions (concepts/agent-oversight-and-delegation).
  • People. Whether employees disclose their AI use follows trust, not policy: 14 % hide it in high-trust teams, 47 % in low-trust ones (concepts/ai-knowledge-hiding).

2.4 After the session

A student who opens the card page can reach, in one tap from each card, the wiki page or visualisation behind it.

3 · Output qualities — what good looks like

3a. Invariants — pass/fail, every card

  • Within the slide-deck limits: a headline of 12 words at most, a body of 40 words at most, 3 bullets at most, no sentence over 20 words.
  • Clean under strip-ai-language: no warm-ups, empty intensifiers or future promises, every antithesis either rewritten or kept with a written reason.
  • Every factual claim traceable to a named AI Wiki page.
  • Nothing on a card or in a note that would tell a student, before the experiment, which way its hypothesis points.

3b. Qualities — judged on the segment as a whole

  • The three headlines, read alone, tell the argument: an agent is something you already use; the harness is the part you own; trust splits into data, output and people.
  • A student leaves with the words the week 4–6 layers will need, and without the answers those weeks are meant to discover.

4 · Known constraints

  • The experiment comes first. The week 3 session runs the information-seeking experiment, in which an agent appears in one round. Its protocol names demand characteristics as the biggest threat and asks that the hypothesis direction is not stated beforehand. An agents segment placed before the experiment is a manipulation of it.
  • The card page is public before the session. Week pages go up about a week ahead. Anything on a card is read by some students before the experiment, whatever the running order on the day.
  • No image pipeline. The decks carry text-shaped evidence. The two visualisations are linked and opened live.
  • The deck builder’s parser accepts <section class="slide…" id="sN"> and nothing else, so the segment is identified by its kicker text.
  • The presenter build needs .venv with python-pptx, which does not exist on this machine as of 18 September 2026.

6 · Open questions and assumptions

  • Where exactly in the 150 minutes. Assumed: straight after the experiment debrief, before team work. Owner: the AIBS owner.
  • Whether card 3 refers to the room’s own round with the agent. After the debrief it can, and the link is the strongest one available. It belongs in the note, not on the card, for the reason in §4. Owner: the AEL owner.
  • Who builds the rest of site/week-03-slides.html. The segment shares the file; see the build plan’s parallelisation section.
  • Assumption: the week 2 debrief did name the harness and ReAct, as the LRD schedules. If it did not, card 2 introduces them rather than recalling them, and the note says so.
