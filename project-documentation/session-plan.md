# Week 1 — session plan

One joint AEL + AIBS block, ~4 hours (240 min, one break). Both lecturers present. Teams are formed in this session.

**Goal of the session:** students leave in a team of four, with a shared understanding of where the module is headed (the handbook), and a first draft started on both week-1 deliverables. We leave with a read on where the group's starting level is.

**Pre-reading (light):** the European Commission's short summary of the Draghi report, *The future of European competitiveness* (Sept 2024) — <https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-competitiveness/draghi-report_en>. One page is enough; nobody needs to read all 400.

---

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:20 — Welcome and how the module runs

- The minor, and the two modules as one pipeline: AEL builds a tool; AIBS decides what the tool's output must achieve and judges it. Same teams do both.
- The scope: **6 weeks of classes, then an assessment.**
- The goal: a **handbook about the state of AI for SMEs** — what a regional SME owner needs to know and decide. One new published page per team per week.
- How we're running it: **week by week**. We start basic, see how it goes, decide the next step together. What's fixed (end goal, teams, weekly page, two gates, final interview) vs. what we decide as we go.

### 0:20–0:55 — Discussion 1: your experience with AI

Structured round, capture answers on the board. This is also our diagnostic — we use it to plan the coming weeks.

- Which AI tools have you actually used, and for what?
- Where has it clearly helped you? Where has it failed or misled you?
- Confidence check (hands / 1–5): *using* AI tools · *building* something with AI · *judging* whether an AI output is any good.
- How do you think AI will change the job market you're about to enter — for your field specifically? Optimistic, worried, both?
- What skills do you think you'll need to start well on a job market where AI is a given? Which of those do you feel you have, and which are gaps?
- What do you want to be able to do by the end of this minor?

Keep a written record of the confidence spread, the job-market / skills answers, and the "want to be able to do" answers.

### 0:55–1:35 — Discussion 2: the Draghi report

**Framing (≈10 min, lecturer):**

- What it is: a report by Mario Draghi for the European Commission (Sept 2024) on why the EU is falling behind the US and China on growth and productivity.
- Core diagnosis: the gap is largely a **technology gap**. Europe is weak in digital/tech, has few large tech firms, and — crucially — is slow to *diffuse* digital technology into its existing businesses, especially SMEs.
- On AI: Europe won't win only by trying to build frontier models; it has to get AI *adopted* across its industrial base, including small and mid-sized firms.
- Barriers Draghi names for SMEs: fragmented single market, regulatory complexity, limited access to capital, skills shortages, limited scale.
- Why this is our starting point: the handbook is a small, concrete contribution to exactly that diffusion problem — helping regional SMEs understand and act on AI.

**Guided discussion (≈25 min):**

- Do you recognise the "Europe is behind" framing? Think about the AI tools you named earlier — who makes them?
- Draghi treats the productivity gap as essentially a tech gap. What would it actually take for one local SME to close its own gap?
- The report is about competitiveness. What does that lens underweight — trust, dependency on foreign vendors, jobs, data?
- If you were advising a regional SME tomorrow, what is the *first* thing they'd need to know about AI?

The last question is the bridge into the assignments.

### 1:35–1:45 — Break

### 1:45–2:10 — Team formation

- Teams of four.
- Each member takes one AEL build role: **product manager · developer · tester · deployer**. Everyone also researches in AIBS.
- Quick sanity check per team: four people, four roles, at least one person willing to drive the CLI.

### 2:10–2:25 — The two assignments

Hand out both prompts (`assignment-ael-prd.md`, `assignment-aibs-research-proposal.md`) and explain how they fit together:

- **AEL — Product Requirements Document:** what the tool your team builds should be like.
- **AIBS — research proposal:** who the handbook is for, what they need, the questions it must answer, and the quality criteria it must meet.
- They constrain each other: the research proposal says what "good" means; the PRD says how the tool will get there. Draft them side by side.

### 2:25–3:40 — Team work time

Teams start both drafts. Both lecturers circulate — AEL lecturer on the PRD, AIBS lecturer on the proposal. Push every team to name a concrete target SME (sector, size, region) as early as possible.

### 3:40–4:00 — Share-out and close

- Each team says one sentence: *who our handbook is for* — and *one open question we're stuck on*.
- **Introduce the individual portfolio** (ADR-0011): one document per student, added to every week, carrying **30 %** of each of their two marks — the interview carries the other 70 %. Say the four standard questions (what the team published · what *you* contributed · what the gates asked and what you changed · what your build role produced), and say that week 1's entry is shorter because there is no page and no gate yet: what you contributed, what your role did first, and **what you do not understand yet**. Half a page.
  - Two things to say out loud, because they are what the instrument lives or dies on: **write it the same week, not at the end** — an assembled-from-memory portfolio is visible as one; and **an honest small contribution scores better than an unsupportable large one**, because the portfolio makes the claim and the interview tests it.
  - Say explicitly that **the two weekly gates are still not scored**. Students will assume otherwise now that something weekly carries a mark, and the gates stop working the moment they are read as grading.
- Next steps: where drafts get submitted, when, and that next week is decided based on what we saw today — partly from the *what I don't understand yet* answers.
