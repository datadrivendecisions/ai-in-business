# Course outline — AIBS + AEL (integrated)

**Modules:** AI in Business & Society (AIBS, research — Meike Botterweg) and Agent Engineering Lab (AEL, technical — Witek ten Hove), run as one integrated pipeline inside HAN's Data Driven Decision Making minor.

**North star (the ideal, not the plan):** the full design lives in the Learning Requirement Document — <https://datadrivendecisions.github.io/ai-in-business/integrated-lrd.html>. That document describes what we want in the ideal situation. This outline describes how we are actually going to run it.

---

## How we're running it: adaptive, week by week

We are **not** building all the materials in advance. We start the first class with basic assignments, watch how students respond and where their starting level is, and then decide week by week what comes next. We'll see how far we get.

### What is fixed

- **The end goal.** Each team produces a *handbook* about the state of AI aimed at SMEs: what a regional SME owner needs to know and decide about AI. AEL builds the tool that produces it; AIBS defines what it must achieve and judges whether it does.
- **The scope.** 6 weeks of classes, then an assessment period.
- **Teams of four.** Every student holds one build role in AEL (product manager / developer / tester / deployer) and does research in AIBS. Same people build and research.
- **One published page per team per week.** A new handbook page on that week's theme — never an edit to a previous page — published to the shared site.
- **Two gates per week**, both informational, never scored:
  - *Socratic gate* — an AI tutor questions the team's thinking (instructor-owned, one submission per week).
  - *Peer gate* — a partner team reviews the page against shared criteria.

  Neither gate carries a mark. What a student *did* with a gate's questions is recorded in their portfolio, and that is assessable; the gate itself is not an instrument.
- **An individual portfolio.** Each student keeps one document, added to every week, recording the team's published page, their own contribution to it, what the gates asked and what they changed as a result, and what their build role produced. It carries **30 % of each module's mark**. (ADR-0011.)
- **Week 1 deliverables.** AEL: a first-draft Product Requirements Document. AIBS: a first-draft research proposal. Both teams also start the individual portfolios. (See `week-01/`.)
- **The finish.** A consolidated handbook plus one joint criterion-based interview with both lecturers, two independent marks. **The interview carries 70 % of each mark and the individual portfolio 30 %.** Evidence base: the team's frozen submissions, its gate/iteration history, and each student's portfolio.

### What we decide week by week

- How much scaffolding each assignment needs (worked examples, templates, checklists) — driven by what we see in class 1.
- The exact sub-theme and depth of each weekly page.
- Whether to run the field visit as planned (weeks 2–4) or adjust.
- How far we get through the provisional week map below.

## Week 1 (detailed)

One joint AEL + AIBS block, ~4 hours. Teams are formed during this class.

1. Discussion: students' knowledge of and experience with AI (also our diagnostic for the weeks ahead).
2. Discussion: the Draghi report on European competitiveness — why AI diffusion into SMEs matters.
3. Team formation.
4. Kick off both assignments.

Materials: `week-01/session-plan.md`, `week-01/assignment-ael-prd.md`, `week-01/assignment-aibs-research-proposal.md`.

## Teaching-team decision log

We keep a short running note, updated each week: what we observed about the students' level, what we'll do next week as a result, and what we're adding or dropping versus the LRD ideal. First entry after class 1.
