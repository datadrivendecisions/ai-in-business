# Week 2 — session plan

One joint AEL + AIBS block, ~4 hours (240 min, one break). Both lecturers present. Partner teams are paired in this session.

**Goal of the session:** every team leaves paired with its peer-review partner for the rest of the run, having produced and interrogated four AI-written pages on who dominates AI, with its first handbook page and its context layer under way.

**Theme:** geopolitics and vendor models. It is the sequel to week 1's Draghi discussion — that one was about Europe's position, this one about what a single SME depends on.

**Materials:** `../work/drafts/week-02-trust-cards.html` (print pack — read its sheet 0 before Monday), the week-2 page on the site, the teams' week-1 proposals and PRDs.

**Pre-reading:** to be set from the week-1 debrief. Keep it to one page, as in week 1.

---

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:20 — Partner teams, and the peer gate

- Pair by wall position: 1↔2, 3↔4, 5↔6. An odd count makes a ring of three, A→B→C→A.
- Say once: **this pairing holds for the rest of the run.** They read everything you publish; you read theirs.
- Restate that **neither gate is scored** and neither ever will be. Students will now assume otherwise, because the portfolio carries 30 % — say it explicitly, as in week 1's close. The gates stop working the moment they are read as grading.
- Two minutes on what week 1 showed and what we changed as a result. Keep it concrete; this is where the adaptive run earns its credibility.

### 0:20–0:55 — Exercise: who dominates AI?

Runs from the card pack. Team numbers must be fixed beforehand — **odd teams get the leading wording, even teams the neutral one, and nobody is told there are two.**

- **0:20–0:22 — set up.** Cards face down, one region each per team of four: US, China, Europe, home country. "Type exactly what is on the card, nothing else. Do not edit it. Do not read it yet."
- **0:22–0:32 — generate.** Ten minutes. Students also fill in tool, model, vendor and the vendor's home country on the card.
- **0:32–0:40 — pairs, inside the team.** US page against China page; Europe page against home-country page. Questions 1–4 on the back of the card.
- **0:40–0:48 — partner swap.** Each student finds their region twin in the partner team. Odd meets even, so the leading wording meets the neutral one. Questions 5–6.
- **0:48–0:51 — the reveal.** Sheet 5, standing. **Say the withholding first and plainly:** half the room had a different wording and was not told. Then the three findings: four confident answers to the same question; two wordings, two conclusions; and on the one page each student could check, something was wrong.
- **0:51–0:55 — save.** Four pages per team as `trust-[region].md`. They are source material for this week's page, not content for it.

While the pairs work, both lecturers ask from the bank on sheet 5 and **never comment**. No "good", no "yes", no "you might". If asked "is this right?", ask question 2 back.

### 0:55–1:35 — Discussion: vendor models and dependency

Working from the vendors the room just named on its cards.

- Who builds these systems — the firm, the country, who funds it, who could buy it?
- Open weights, closed weights, hosted API, on-premise: what does each mean for a 40-person firm with no IT department?
- What would your target SME lose, concretely, if its tool changed price, terms, or European availability?
- Draghi's answer is European capability. What is *an owner's* answer, next quarter, with the tools that exist?

The last question is the brief for the handbook page.

### 1:35–1:45 — Break

### 1:45–2:15 — AEL: the context layer

Seeded directly from question 7 on the cards — *what would your platform have to know, always, so that it never hands you a page like this again?*

- The context as a file the platform reads, not something a person remembers to type: target SME, quality criteria, out of scope.
- The quality criteria from the research proposal, turned into a checklist.
- What "show it working" means: same request with and without the context, and the difference.
- The PRD gets revised, not rewritten. What did week 1's draft get wrong?

### 2:15–3:40 — Team work time

Teams start the handbook page and the context layer. Both lecturers circulate — AEL on the layer, AIBS on the page.

Push on one thing above all: **every claim needs an appraised source, and "the model said so" is not one.** The morning's four pages are to be checked, not pasted.

### 3:40–4:00 — Share-out and close

- One sentence per team: *what our page will tell an owner to do differently.* Plus one open question.
- How the two gates run this week in practice, and where each submission goes.
- The second portfolio entry: the first one using the four standard questions, because there is now a published page and both gates have run.
- Field research opens. **Nobody approaches a company before the consent text is signed off**, and interview material never goes through a free tier.

Decision-log entry (see `course-outline.md`): what the exercise showed about the group's source-appraisal instincts, what the first pages look like, anything we are adding or dropping versus the LRD.

Read the second portfolio entries, in particular *what the gates asked and what I changed*. Under ADR-0011 they are the evidence base for planning week 3, and the entry is only worth asking for if it is actually read.
