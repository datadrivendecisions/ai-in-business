What the mix does to a team of four — four things the site is silent on. None belongs to a single student, which is why none appears in a single journey.

1 · The course is in English; the field research is not

Unstated, it resolves the obvious way: the Dutch members interview, the international members wait outside. Two of four then have no field data of their own.

2 · The source base is Dutch

CBS, the Chamber of Commerce, sector bodies, the regional press. The platform the team builds in AEL is exactly what could retrieve and translate them — the site names neither problem nor route.

3 · Nobody says who finds the partner firm

If it is the team’s job, the search runs on personal networks and the students who have one carry it. If it is the school’s job, one sentence removes the problem.

4 · Teams form themselves, with no rule

Twenty-five minutes of self-selection sorts by who can already talk to whom. “Do not form a team in advance” stops pre-formed friend groups and does nothing about sorting in the room.

What changed — all ten shipped across two cycles; the floor moved 38 → 56 / 60

1

Publish every date the module already knowsSix sessions, the hand-in rule, the assessment window. Lifts both floor personas for unrelated reasons. One paragraph.

2

Say who finds the partner — and how a non-Dutch speaker takes partInterpreter, recording plus platform transcription, or an English-speaking firm. Two sentences at the trough.

3

One worked example of a target SMESector, headcount, region, and the question it is actually asking. It would raise every team’s first proposal.

4

Fix the handbook template’s dead endGive it the same nav bar and point its wordmark home. The smallest edit here, and the only outright inconsistency.

5

Make Dutch-language retrieval an explicit AEL requirementThe one edit that turns a diversity problem into the platform’s clearest use case rather than an accommodation for it.

6

State the course language, and add one team-composition ruleSo that twenty-five minutes of self-selection does not sort the room by who can already talk to whom.

Read down the columns, not only across the rows. The two Dutch personas average 39 and the two international personas 40 — indistinguishable. What the totals hide is that the reasons differ: at the stage-04 trough Fenna and Joris are short of dates and a contact route, while Rohan and Giulia are short of a language and a network. Score the totals and the mix looks handled; read the cells and two of the four gaps belong specifically to the international half of the room, and are named nowhere on the site.

Full instrument, evidence and caveats: work/drafts/course-site-journeys.md. Two residuals are not site edits: the two criterion sets are still unwritten, and the six session dates are derived from the recorded kickoff rather than confirmed. These four students do not exist and were written by the person who built the site — the score that counts is the one a real student gives when they cannot find something.
