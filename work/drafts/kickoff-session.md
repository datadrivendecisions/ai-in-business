# Combined kickoff — AIBS and AEL, week 1

*Lecturer-facing run-of-show for the first contact block of both modules. Draft; not for
students. Written against the [integrated LRD](../../site/integrated-lrd.html) v1.2 — Part 8
(week 1), §6.7 (the contact block) and §6.3 (the gate).*

> **Note.** The operative document for Monday is [kickoff-programme.md](kickoff-programme.md), scored against [kickoff-scorecard.md](kickoff-scorecard.md). This file keeps the earlier run-of-show for its formation mechanism, Socratic bank, alternatives and risks.

> One handbook, two modules, one team of four: in AIBS the team researches it as independent
> researchers, in AEL the same team builds the research platform it researches with, and the
> team only advances when an AI Socratic tutor and a peer team both agree the thinking behind
> the week's contribution — not just the output — is real.
> — LRD, opening

## What the session has to deliver

The LRD's week-1 row: *"Applied research and the problem analysis. Teams form and take the
researcher role. Methodology: problem analysis, internal and external analysis, source
appraisal, designing field research; consent and anonymisation for interviews settled here,
before anyone is approached. The intern analogy as the shared vocabulary."* The question every
team answers this week: **Which SME problem are we researching, for whom, and why does it
matter?** AEL's week-1 focus: agent fundamentals, a platform skeleton, and the research track's
needs captured as a specification. The week-1 gate is the problem analysis.

Every team leaves the room with:

- a formed team of four with the four build roles allocated, everyone also a researcher;
- a peer partner team, fixed for the whole run;
- a first, revised answer to the week-1 question, on paper;
- five user stories for the platform they do not yet have;
- a clear picture of the weekly rhythm, the two gate conditions, and what is due next Monday.

## Starting conditions

- **120 minutes**, one room, both lecturers present.
- **Teams are not formed.** Both modules are mandatory for every student, so every team carries
  all four build roles.
- **Ready:** the shared quality criteria (LRD §6.2). **Not ready:** the four spine wiki pages,
  the SME pool with consent terms, the Socratic agent, the peer-partner assignment. The
  research-platform specification is meant to be captured from the research track in week 1,
  so its absence is the material of one session block rather than a gap.
- Cohort size unknown; the plan is sized for 20 students in five teams and says what changes
  at 16 and 24.
- The modules were recently renamed. Some students will know the old names. Say it once, in
  person, and never again; every hand-out carries the new names only.

## Design principles

1. **Do the loop, don't describe it.** Teams run one miniature round of Questioning →
   Generating → Critiquing → Refining on the week-1 question. The two lecturers play the
   Socratic agent by hand; the peer partner is gate B. The rhythm is named *after* students
   have felt it.
2. **The researchers are the users in the room.** After forty minutes of research on paper, the
   product manager and developer elicit from their teammates what they needed and did not have.
   The missing platform becomes the week-1 specification, authored by its own users.
3. **Content is cheap, insight is the currency.** Open with ninety seconds of "advice" every
   student can produce; spend the session showing why that is not what gets assessed; hand the
   cards back at the debrief.
4. **Constraint first, choice second, for team formation.** A readiness line and a count-off
   guarantee every team can staff a developer; a short swap window gives autonomy back without
   breaking the constraint.
5. **Honest about readiness, strict about consent.** "The Socratic agent and the wiki arrive in
   week 2; this week, we are it." Everything today uses personas. Nobody approaches a real firm
   until the research lecturer has signed the consent text — NFR-09 says *settled before the
   first interview*, and this session is where it is settled.

## Team formation

About twenty minutes including roles and partners.

1. **Self-declaration on entry.** A coloured dot: green = *I can write working Python today*;
   amber = *willing to ramp fast in weeks 1–2*; red = *no coding*. A letter sticker: B (business
   background) or T (IT background). Self-declared, unchecked.
2. **The line (3′).** Along a wall, from most Python to least; greens left, reds right, ambers
   self-sort between. Once, to the line: "If you shipped Python in the last year, step left."
3. **Count-off (2′).** Technical counts 1 … T along the line and restarts at 1, where T is the
   number of teams. Every team gets one person from each readiness band. Teams go to their
   numbered table.
4. **Constraint check (1′).** Research checks every table: at least one green, at least one B
   and one T. A failing table is fixed by swapping with the adjacent one.
5. **Swap window (3′).** Students may swap for any reason; a swap stands only if both tables
   still satisfy the constraint. Lecturer arbitrates. No discussion past three minutes.
6. **Roles (6′).** The developer is a green. The product manager is whoever is happiest talking
   to an owner. The deployer is at least amber. Tester by the team's choice.

T = ⌊N/4⌋; the remainder goes into teams of five (double the tester or the developer), never
teams of three.

- **N = 16** — four teams, two partner pairs.
- **N = 17–19** — four teams, one to three of them with five members.
- **N = 20** — five teams, two pairs and one review ring of three.
- **N = 24** — six teams, three pairs; Socratic visits become three per lecturer at three
  minutes each, which still fits the ten-minute slot.
- **Fewer greens than teams** — the real risk. Reduce T until every team has a green, even if
  that means five per team throughout. Decide that threshold before the session, not in front
  of the room, and say it plainly rather than quietly seating an amber developer.

## The miniature loop — exact prompts

**Questioning (6′), printed on the card:**

> Before you choose a problem for this owner, write at least ten questions you would need
> answered. Mark each K — we could find out from our desk — or O — only the owner can tell us.

**Generating (8′), fixed frame, black pen:**

> We research **[problem]** for **[whom — a role, not "the SME"]** because **[why it matters —
> to them, in their words]**. We know this because **[source or persona line]**. We would have
> to ask the owner: **[one question]**.

**Critiquing, Socratic agent by hand.** Ask three, follow up once, walk away, never comment.

1. Who, by role, loses money or sleep if this problem stays unsolved? How do you know?
2. Point at each claim on your card. Which ones do you know, and which are you assuming?
3. If the owner reads your answer on Monday morning, what does she do by Friday?
4. Why this problem and not the one next to it? What did you reject, and why?
5. What evidence would show you are wrong?
6. Which of your ten questions can you answer without the owner? Why haven't you?
7. Whose data would you need to look at to answer this, and who has agreed to that?

Rules the two lecturers agree before the room fills: no "good", no "yes", no "you might
consider", no nodding as approval. If a team asks "is this right?", answer with question 2. The
other lecturer listens for drift and intervenes. This bank is also a usable seed for the
week-2 Socratic agent's prompt.

**Critiquing, partner channel (3′):**

> Read your partner team's v1 in silence. Write exactly two questions on stickies — questions,
> not advice, not praise. Hand the card back. No conversation.

**Refining (8′), red pen:**

> Rewrite the frame as v2. Strike through what you changed. Add one line: *what changed and
> why*. Then hand the card to your partner team.

**Mini-gate, partner team (2′):**

> ☐ We see genuine revision — the thinking moved, not just the words.
> ☐ Not yet, because: ______

## What the week-1 gate requires

Stated at 1:38 and printed on the *Rhythm & gate* one-pager. The problem analysis, one page:

- the problem, for whom (a role), and why it matters to them;
- an outline of the internal and external analysis still to be done;
- every claim carrying an appraised source — who says it, and why we trust them;
- the field-interview design: an interview guide and draft consent and anonymisation text;
- evidence of the peer exchange and of revision in response to it.

Gate A this week is the two lecturers reading the Socratic transcript by hand; gate B is the
partner team's review, given and received. Both, or the gate stays shut. A shut gate is not a
penalty; the work carries into week 2.

## Homework by next Monday

Three lines, read aloud, printed with the consent rule in bold.

1. **Problem analysis v3**, one page: problem, for whom, why; internal and external analysis
   outline; every claim with an appraised source. Persona or desk research only.
2. **Field-interview design**: interview guide plus draft consent and anonymisation text.
   **Nobody approaches a real firm until the research lecturer has signed the text.**
3. **Partner exchange**: v3 to your partner by Thursday, three questions back by Saturday, v4
   with visible revision on Monday. Developer and product manager turn the five user stories
   into issues in the skeleton repository.

## Pre-session preparation

One page each, no slide decks.

- **Persona cards, three.** A 30-person CNC machining shop; a family metal-fabrication firm; a
  60-person machine-builder with a service desk. Each: the owner, headcount, one pain, one
  number, one thing already tried, and the line *this firm does not exist; do not go looking
  for it*. One per team plus spares.
- **Role card.** Product manager, developer, tester, deployer — one paragraph each — plus
  *everyone is a researcher* and *the developer is a green*.
- **A3 team card.** Team name, four names, roles, readiness colours, partner team.
- **A3 problem-analysis card.** Questions box; v1 frame; Socratic-questions box; sticky area;
  v2 frame; *what changed and why*; the gate tick box.
- **Spec card.** *The platform we don't have* — five lines of *As a researcher I need … so that …*.
- **Rhythm & gate one-pager.** The LRD's opening sentence; the six weekly themes; gate A and
  gate B; what the week-1 gate requires; the assessment sentence; *no scores*. Quality criteria
  on the reverse.
- **Homework sheet**, as above.
- **Materials.** Green, amber and red dots; B and T stickers; black and red pens per table;
  stickies; index cards; a timer the whole room can see.
- **Agreed between the lecturers beforehand.** The team-count threshold when greens are fewer
  than teams. Who keeps time (Technical). The Socratic no-comment rule, rehearsed once on each
  other. The one-sentence answer to "where is the Socratic agent, where is the wiki?" The
  one-sentence name-change line. A shared folder for the card photos. A skeleton repository URL
  for the developers, or an honest date. The status of the consent text.

## Swap-in alternatives

1. **Persona panel instead of Questioning.** Both lecturers sit at the front in character as
   two SME owners; teams have eight minutes to interview them. Costs two minutes from
   Generating. Prefer for a business-heavy cohort — it rehearses the field interview they must
   design this week. Depends on the lecturers holding character and refusing to answer what
   the owner would not know.
2. **Intern briefing instead of requirements elicitation.** Each team briefs a chatbot as an
   intern to produce a problem analysis for its persona, scores the result against the quality
   criteria, and writes the three things the intern got wrong. Fifteen minutes. Prefer when AI
   literacy is low; the intern analogy becomes physical. Avoid if devices or accounts are
   uncertain — it fails loudly.
3. **The clunky platform simulation.** Run Refining under a deliberate constraint: v2 may only
   reach the partner through a lecturer who reads it aloud, and Socratic questions arrive on
   paper five minutes late. Five-minute debrief: "that was the cost of no platform." Prefer for
   an IT-heavy cohort that might regard the platform as a trivial CRUD app.
4. **Gallery walk instead of paired critique.** Every team walks the wall and leaves one
   question on every other card. Prefer when there are six or more teams, or a late arrival
   delays the pairing. Weaker on gate B, because *received* becomes diffuse; keep the mini-gate
   tick with the fixed partner regardless.
5. **Role speed-dating for formation.** Four three-minute rounds in which students pitch which
   role they want and why, then self-select under the same constraint. About twenty minutes,
   so it eats the roles block. Only when the cohort is sixteen or fewer and already knows each
   other.

## Risks specific to this session

- **Size and readiness unknown until the door opens.** Count dots at 0:14 before the line-up;
  the threshold is pre-decided, so Technical only reads it off.
- **Socratic-by-hand drifts into advice.** Printed bank, rehearsed, and the other lecturer
  polices. Ask only from the bank.
- **A team gets *not yet* in public.** Framed as normal before Refining; never singled out in
  the debrief.
- **Personas read as licence to contact a real firm.** The consent rule at 0:34, at 1:48, on
  the homework sheet and on the persona card.
- **"Where is the wiki, the agent, the spec?"** Answered once, honestly, at 1:38. The
  elicitation block turns the missing spec into the students' own week-1 output, so the absence
  reads as intended.
- **Old module names.** One sentence at 0:06, never repeated; hand-outs carry the new names only.
- **Energy drop.** Students are on their feet at 0:14, 0:58 and 1:55; the break at 1:20 is
  fixed. If the room sags earlier, take it at 1:16 and cut open questions to match.
- **Overrun.** See the cut order under the run-of-show.

## Open points for the module owners

- Which lecturer signs off the consent text, and by when — the homework assumes it exists by
  the end of week 1.
- Whether the week-1 gate is graded by hand from the Socratic transcripts, as the LRD's roadmap
  proposes for the pilot, or waived for week 1 while the agent is built.
- Whether the shared folder for card photos is the skeleton repository itself, so that the
  spec cards become the first issues without a second hand-over.
