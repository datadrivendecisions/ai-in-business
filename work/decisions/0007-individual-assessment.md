# ADR-0007: Assessing individuals when the deliverable is one collective handbook

## Status

Superseded by ADR-0011

## Context

Under ADR-0001 the module's deliverable is a handbook, and under ADR-0002 week 7
is spent merging every team's contribution into a single document published under
the programme's name. The research lecturer's proposal identifies the problem
this creates directly: once the contributions are merged, individual teams'
work may no longer be recognisable in the result, and the suggestion is that
teams submit the final version of their own contribution separately.

The assessment is a criterion-based interview whose input is *the handbook and/or
the team's contribution to it* — so the artefact under discussion is partly
collective, while the mark is individual. Underneath the team-versus-individual
question sits a second one, since the interview is the only place an individual
claim can actually be tested.

A further proposal is that students reflect in the interview on how the knowledge
presented in the handbook is relevant to professionals in each of the four build
roles they held — product manager, developer, tester, deployer — so that they
think about who in a company needs to know what. That reflection is also a
candidate assessment instrument, and the underlying question of role-specific
knowledge is a handbook theme in its own right.

ADR-0005 sharpens the problem: field research is a team obligation, so a student
can pass without ever having conducted an interview.

## Options considered

**Mark the collective handbook.** Simple and true to the collaborative reality.
Indefensible individually, and it rewards free-riding in a way that is visible to
the cohort and corrosive.

**Mark the team contribution, submitted separately before the merge.** Preserves
attribution and gives the interview a concrete object. Still a team mark, so
within-team variation is invisible unless the interview surfaces it.

**Mark the individual entirely on the interview, with the contribution as
input.** Defensible individually and matches how a CBI is meant to work — the
artefact is evidence, the interview establishes what the student can do with it.
The risk is a mark that floats free of the work, rewarding students who present
well.

**Individual portfolio alongside the collective handbook.** Each student keeps a
record of what they contributed and what they learned. Strong attribution, and a
substantial extra workload for students and markers alike.

## Decision

Teams submit their own contribution separately before the merge, and that
submission is the evidence base for an individual criterion-based interview which
carries the mark.

The separate submission solves attribution at the team level and gives the
interview something specific to interrogate. The mark stays individual and stays
on the interview, because that is the only instrument in the design capable of
distinguishing between two members of the same team.

The role-relevance reflection is adopted as a standard part of the interview: the
student is asked which of the four build roles needs which of the handbook's
findings, and why. It tests transfer rather than recall, it connects the two
tracks in the way ADR-0003 requires the joint interview to, and it cannot be
answered from a contribution the student did not help write.

Assessment criteria remain to be written; this record fixes the instrument and
its evidence base, not the rubric.

## Consequences

- The merge in week 7 must happen after the team submissions are frozen, which
  constrains the order of work in a week that is already tight.
- Two versions of every contribution exist — the submitted one and the merged one
  — and the difference between them is editorial, not authorial. That must be
  clear to students, or the merge will be read as marking.
- Interview questions must be individual, not addressed to the team. Interviewing
  a team in one room produces a team impression.
- A student who contributed little to a good contribution can still be marked
  down, and the criteria must say so explicitly or the outcome will be
  contested.
- The role-relevance reflection presumes the four build roles from the technical
  module, so a student taking AIBS without the sister module needs an
  alternative question. Under ADR-0003 the modules are formally separate, so this
  case will occur.
- Combined with ADR-0005, a student may hold a mark for research skills without
  having conducted an interview. Either the interview probes the fieldwork
  specifically, or the criteria stop claiming the skill.
